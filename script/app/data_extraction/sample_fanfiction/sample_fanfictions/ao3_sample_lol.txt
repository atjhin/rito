"There." Darius pointed to an emergency exit at the far end. The red paint had mostly flaked off, but the door looked intact. The handle hadn't rusted through yet.

They made it halfway before the floor gave out. Just enough that Mel's leg went through, punched straight down into the purple sludge below. It burned immediately, eating through her pants like acid. She shrieked, couldn't help it, and clawed at the solid floor. Her nails scraped wood. The burning spread. White flashed behind Mel’s eyes. 

Darius grabbed her arms and hauled her up. The purple substance came with her, clinging like honey, like tar. He ripped off a piece of his shirt and took four seconds wiping it away. Four seconds that felt eternal. Four seconds that might have damned them both.

"Can you walk?" His face was close to hers.

Mel tested her weight. Something monstrous crawled beneath her skin, burning through muscle and bone. "Yes. I can keep up." She could hear the hive already, murmuring to her. 

"Bullshit." But he pulled her arm over his shoulders, taking most of her weight. "Move."

Getting to the exit was difficult. They couldn't risk the floor completely giving in, not with that acid below. Mel pushed her weight with her good leg. When she gave Darius a little nod, he moved. Behind them, the front door finally gave way with a crash that shook the whole building. The horde poured through. 

The dog ran at them. 

"No!" Darius whistled. "Here! Now!"

The dog broke off, backing toward them, snapping at any shambler that got close. A few times the floor almost dropped below it, but the dog was agile, quick, reading the weak spots like it could see through wood. Its muzzle was black with infected blood. Mel prayed none of it had gotten in its mouth.

Darius kicked the emergency exit. The first time it didn’t budge. The second time it flew open and they spilled out into an alley choked with garbage and fungus. The dog ran through after them. Darius slammed the door shut and jammed a piece of rebar through the push-bar, bending the metal until it held. Pilled up bodies hit the other side immediately.

Mel shut her eyes when she heard the floor break, the monsters scream as they fell into the acid, the liquid gurgle as it consumed them. The smell that came through the door was unspeakable.

"This way." Darius was already moving, still supporting Mel's weight. He towered over her, so had to bend to keep her arm over his shoulders. 

"I'm slowing you down," Mel said through gritted teeth. The pain in her leg was spreading, crawling up toward her hip. Mel remembered the deal. “You don’t have to–”

"Shut up."

"Keep going without me. I can't…"

"Shut up."

Her vision blurred. The last thing Mel Medarda saw was the dog looking up at her, black blood dripping from it’s fur.
